    Five ways to break it

    Each failure runs at 10% to 90% of the corpus or query traffic. The harmless changes measure how often a detector raises a false alarm.

    Failures

    • F1 Partial embedding upgrade: two models’ vectors in one indexOct 31
    • F2 Stale documents: outdated content beside its replacementOct 31
    • F3 Chunking change on part of the corpusOct 31
    • F4 Query shift toward topics the corpus lacksOct 31
    • F5 Config mismatch between index and queriesOct 31

    What the severity means

    At 30%, 1,089 of the 3,633 document vectors come from the other model. Queries, the index size and the error count stay exactly the same, which is the whole problem.

    How harm is decided

    A window counts as harmful when its nDCG@10 drop from the clean index is significant under a one-sided paired bootstrap at α = 0.05. Detectors never see this label; it only grades them.

    Where the project stands

    The pipeline, the experiment runner and the baseline are done. With multi-qa-MiniLM-L6-cos-v1, title and text cut at 256 tokens, a window of 100 queries lets the paired bootstrap catch an nDCG@10 drop of about 0.06 in 80% of windows.

    Numbers on this page come from a prototype of F1. The real injectors and detectors land over the next four weeks, then everything runs once on SciFact and FiQA, which nothing is tuned on. The detector-by-failure heatmap will replace this section.
